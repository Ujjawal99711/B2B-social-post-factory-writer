Post 1:
Before arguing whether your business deserves 8× or 10× EBITDA, check whether the promoter salary distorting the Valuation?

Reported EBITDA = ₹5 crore
Promoter salary = ₹2 crore
Replacement CEO cost = ₹60 lakh
Valuation multiple = 8×

A buyer does not simply value:
₹5 crore × 8 = ₹40 crore

The buyer asks:
What would this business earn under a normal management structure?

Here, the promoter is drawing ₹1.40 crore above market.
So:
₹5 crore + ₹1.40 crore add-back = ₹6.40 crore normalised EBITDA
At 8×: Business value = ₹51.20 crore
Same company. Same year.
₹11.20 crore difference - because the multiple was applied to the wrong earnings base.

Now flip it.
Suppose the promoter draws only ₹20 lakh, while a replacement CEO costs ₹80 lakh.
Reported EBITDA is still ₹5 crore.
But the buyer must recognize another ₹60 lakh of management cost.
₹5 crore − ₹60 lakh = ₹4.40 crore normalised EBITDA
At 8×: Business value = ₹35.20 crore

Promoter salary is not just an accounting expense. It can be a valuation assumption.
- Above-market salary → reported EBITDA may understate earnings
- Below-market salary → reported EBITDA may overstate earnings
- Market salary → reported EBITDA is closer to sustainable earnings

The multiple is the second argument. The earnings base is the first.

Two caveats he stated:
First, an add-back may not survive if the promoter is critical to customers, relationships or operations.
Second, even if normalized EBITDA rises, the multiple may fall if removing the promoter increases business risk.

The real question is not "What EBITDA is reported?" It is "What EBITDA will still exist after ownership changes?"
That is the profit a buyer is really paying for.

Post 2:
One assumption changed and INR 33,000 crore mcap gone.
Stock lost nearly 32% of its Value in a single session today.
PB Fintech, Policy Bazaar it is.
It didn’t lose a customer. Its product didn’t break.
Yesterday, PB Fintech closed at about ₹1,886.
Today, the stock fell as much as 32%, touching an 18-month low after successive lower-circuit moves.
What changed? IRDAI released a draft consultation paper proposing tighter commission limits across insurance distribution.
For health insurance, first-year commissions for distribution entities are proposed at around 15%, with lower renewal and portability commissions.
For pure-term insurance, first-year commissions for distribution entities could go up to 25% in multi-year premium policies.
For PB Fintech, distribution commission is central to the economics of the model.
Bernstein reportedly described the proposed cuts as far more severe than expected, while Jefferies estimated that a 10% reduction in commission rates could translate into roughly a 10–12% earnings decline for PB Fintech and Turtlemint.
A valuation is rarely held up by 100 assumptions. Usually, a few assumptions do most of the work.
For PB Fintech, one of those assumptions is the take rate.
If regulation caps that number, revenue economics, margin assumptions, future cash flows, and the valuation multiple can change.
When he looks at any business, he asks: What is the single assumption that, if it changes tomorrow, can hurt the Valuation the most?
It could be a regulator, a dominant customer, a platform, a key supplier, a take rate, a commodity price, or one major contract.
If someone else controls that assumption, you have a concentration risk.
Caveat he stated: this is still only a draft proposal. IRDAI has invited stakeholder feedback until 25 October 2026, and the final framework could change materially.
The lesson he drew: when one assumption can erase 30% of market value in hours, that assumption was doing far more work in the valuation than most people realised.
Close: What is the one assumption your business cannot afford to lose?
